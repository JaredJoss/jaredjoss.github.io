## Detailed Research & Publications

### Primary Research: LLM Safety & Healthcare

**1. From Text to Voice: How Real-Time Pipelines Change Interruption Recovery in Cascaded Clinical Agents**
- **Venue:** RTCA Workshop @ NeurIPS 2026 (Oral)
- **Summary:** Replays the same clinical interruption in text and through a live cascaded voice pipeline, and finds that one or two words committed after the patient starts speaking can flip the safety verdict.
- **Link:** https://openreview.net/forum?id=ez6dvTCMby

**2. Does the Voice Matter? Evaluating Frustration Detection on Deployed Clinical Voice-Agent Calls**
- **Venue:** ML4H 2026 (Proceedings); also at the GenAI4Health Workshop @ NeurIPS 2026
- **Summary:** Tests per-turn frustration detection on real calls from a deployed clinical voice agent. About 59% of patient turns are one or two words, where transcript-only detection does not exclude chance, and whether audio helps depends on the model and the instruction.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21001

**3. Same Eye, Different Scanner: A Decision-Reliability Benchmark for Frontier VLMs and OCT Foundation Models in Glaucoma**
- **Venue:** VLM4RWD Workshop @ NeurIPS 2026
- **Summary:** Benchmarks whether frontier VLMs and OCT foundation models keep the same glaucoma decision for the same eye when the scanner changes, scoring decision flips, sensitivity change and calibration.
- **Link:** https://openreview.net/forum?id=1I9Pfkv8dY

**4. The Camera, the Split, and the Label: A Three-Check Audit of Glaucoma-Progression Prediction**
- **Venue:** TAE Workshop @ NeurIPS 2026
- **Summary:** Audits glaucoma-progression results on the public GRAPE benchmark with three checks: a device-only baseline, a patient-grouped split and an input-label overlap test. At this sample size, none of the reported numbers can be distinguished from a trivial baseline.
- **Link:** https://openreview.net/forum?id=AJbpzv4COc

**5. When Patients Cut In: Extending Clinical Conversational AI Safety to Interruptions**
- **Venue:** EMNLP 2026 Findings; oral at the DAIH Workshop @ COLM 2026
- **Summary:** Evaluates whether clinical voice agents can recover when patients interrupt mid-utterance, using conversation-analytic interruption categories and LLM judges to score recovery against required clinical content.
- **Link:** https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-emnlp.2456/

**6. When the Language Changes, Is It Still Safe? Multilingual Evaluation of Clinical Voice Agents**
- **Venue:** DAIH Workshop @ COLM 2026
- **Summary:** Adapts MATRIX to English, French, Polish and Urdu. Emergency handling holds in all four languages, while symptom coverage and follow-up degrade unevenly across models and languages.
- **Link:** https://openreview.net/forum?id=mfgdFCAplv

**7. WER is Unaware: Assessing How ASR Errors Distort Clinical Understanding in Patient Facing Dialogue**
- **Venue:** IWSDS 2026 (Oral)
- **Summary:** Finds WER poorly reflects clinical risk from ASR errors and proposes an LLM judge matching clinician safety assessments.
- **Link:** https://aclanthology.org/2026.iwsds-1.39/
- **Code:** https://github.com/Ufonia/wer-is-unaware

**8. MATRIX: Multi-Agent simulaTion fRamework for safe Interactions and conteXtual clinical conversational evaluation**
- **Venue:** arXiv preprint (2025)
- **Summary:** Structured multi-agent framework to evaluate safety and behaviour risks in clinical dialogue agents at scale.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19163

**9. Insights from the Inverse: Reconstructing LLM Training Goals Through Inverse Reinforcement Learning**
- **Venue:** COLM 2025
- **Tech Stack:** TRL, Hugging Face, Pythia (70M/410M), RLHF.
- **Key Findings:** Developed a max-margin Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL) algorithm to extract reward models from fine-tuned LLMs.
  - The algorithm achieved **80.40% accuracy** in recovering the ground-truth objective.
  - Demonstrated that fine-tuning with the extracted IRL reward model resulted in **lower toxicity** than the original RLHF process.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12491

**10. ASTRID — An Automated and Scalable TRIaD for the Evaluation of RAG-based Clinical Question Answering Systems**
- **Venue:** ACL 2025
- **Focus:** RAG Evaluation, Clinical QA, Reliability.
- **Summary:** A framework designed to evaluate Retrieval-Augmented Generation systems in clinical settings, focusing on safety, reliability, and scalability.
- **Link:** https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.857/
